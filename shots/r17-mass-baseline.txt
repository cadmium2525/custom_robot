WHAT COUNTS AS ONE MASS, stated in full because a mass count with an unstated
threshold is instrument fault 14 in another costume:

  1. The machine's pixels come from a depth-correct white-on-black stencil, not
     from colour keying or a hand-drawn box. Contact shadow and blob excluded.
     A machine behind a pillar is measured on the part you can see.
  2. Its luminance is blurred by a MASKED gaussian (three box passes) of
     sigma = body height / 32, so a 283px machine and a 33px machine are
     squinted at with the same RELATIVE acuity. Background pixels carry no
     weight, so the deck never bleeds into an arm.
  3. The blurred luminance is quantised to ABSOLUTE bands of `step` levels in
     0-255, at four phase offsets (0, .25, .5, .75 of a step), and the counts
     averaged. Fixing one phase is worth up to two masses on this model.
  4. One mass = one 4-connected region of a single band, of area >= 3% of that
     machine's stencil area.
  5. THE COUNT IS A CURVE, NOT A NUMBER. step is swept 24..85. m51 (five value
     bands) is the headline because that is the rule the review quoted, but a
     claim only counts if the sign of the change holds across the sweep.
  6. top4% — the share of the body the four largest masses cover — is printed
     beside it, because five masses covering 40% of the machine is not a toy.
     A count that falls while top4 falls is a machine that lost its structure,
     not a machine that gained a read.

The "four or five" this is scored against remains undefined by any rule; it has
never been measured off a Custom Robo V2 frame. That is rank 1 on the review's
own list and it is not this file's to fix. What this file can do is state its
own rule, which it now does.
